# The character

Only when the app has a mascot or character. It is the most expensive thing on a screen: used well it
makes moments feel personal; used everywhere it becomes a sticker. These rules come from rounds on one
app's character (a drawn clapperboard) and another's identity mark (a candle), and hold for any.

## One on a screen, and it visits

- **One character on a screen, ever.** Never a small one in the corner and a big one in the middle.
- **It comes, speaks, acts and goes.** It enters (walks in, hops, rises from below), says one short
  line (one or two lines in a bubble), does its job, and leaves. "It should be able to walk."
- **Never parked.** A character pinned in a corner on every screen is a sticker. It is away while a
  sheet is up.
- **Strategic, not everywhere.** Pick the moments it appears on purpose: the opening, an empty page,
  the AI writing, a success. Most screens don't have it.

## How it looks

- **No glow behind it, no circle or disc around it, no soft blush.** If it has cheeks, they are crisp
  shapes. "I hate the glowy character"; "I don't like the circle around this character".
- **The whole mark, never a fragment.** A candle, not a bare flame; the full figure at its brand state.
- **Sized to act.** Big enough to read its face and moods; one owner asked for about 45% bigger across
  the board. What holds it (a halo, a bubble, a ring of chips) grows with it. Below about 48 pt it only
  blinks.

## Always alive

- It breathes, blinks, and its eyes look around on their own clock. Give each loop a different period
  so the whole never visibly repeats.
- Build it as one rig of parts that move about their joints (a layered vector drawing), so every mood
  is a pose, not a new drawing. Show every mood on one sheet for the owner.
- **Moods swap, they don't fade.** A part that changes tucks away and the new one springs out. A
  cross-fade leaves grey ghosts.
- **Nothing flat flips.** It never mirrors to turn around or to jump; a flipped drawing shows it is a
  cut-out. It jumps straight up.

## When it reacts

- **Real moments only.** A tick and a jump when sign-in succeeds; a fly-in for an offer; writing while
  the AI writes, then a wink; proud on a finished thing. "We are not using it as nicely as we should
  with these little interactions."
- **Never on taps on other controls.** Buttons already move. "If the mascot moves on every tap it kills
  its special, sacred moments."
- **Tapped itself, it answers.** A small reaction, different each time (a boop, a jump, a wiggle), then
  it settles. The tap still reaches any button around it.

## Reduce Motion

It lands in place without walking or jumping; breathing and blinking still play, small and in place.
It never freezes mid-pose. One owner's own phone had Reduce Motion on and the character froze in a
build; test with it on.

## In the app

The character on the board and in the app is the same rig with the same timings. A character that
animates on the board and stands still in the app was one of the most repeated complaints. Put them
side by side in the simulator before calling it done.
