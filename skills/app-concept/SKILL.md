# App concept

Every screen later borrows from this page. If the concept is vague, the opening is vague, the
story is generic, and the paywall sells features instead of a result.

## Produce one page with exactly these parts

1. **The angle, in one sentence.** What the app does differently, said as a result for a person.
   Not a category ("a time tracker") and not a feeling ("be more productive").
   Good: "It tracks your workday for you, so you see where the hours went without starting a timer."
2. **Who it is for.** One person, their day, the moment the app matters to them.
3. **The promise.** What is true for them after a week. Measurable if possible.
4. **The hero object.** The one visual that carries the brand through the flow (a candle, a plant,
   a ring). It will appear on the first screen and the last.
5. **The feature map.** Every feature as one line: what it does for the person. Mark which three
   carry the promise; those are the only ones the opening shows.
6. **What it is not.** The two or three things people will confuse it with, and why it is not
   those. This line stops later screens from selling the wrong product.
7. **Proof the numbers are honest.** Any figure the onboarding will show (hours lost, money saved)
   and the formula behind it. Exaggerate the scale (a decade, a year), never the facts.

## Rules

- Write it in the reviewer's words. If they said "it's not a blocker", the page says so.
- If two features compete for the opening, the one that is unique wins; the other goes to the story.
- The concept page is re-read before every new section of the flow.

## Anti-patterns

- A tagline instead of an angle ("Your time, reimagined").
- A feature list without the person in it.
- Promises the app cannot keep in week one.
