# Style directions

The owner picks the look; Claude never decides it alone. Offer 3–4 directions, each drawn from the brand, each shown
on the same test subjects at the sizes they'll really show. Then the pick and one approved icon become the style
references for every icon after.

## Where a direction comes from

Read these before writing any direction, and name in each direction which of them it comes from:

- **The character**, if the app has one: its shapes, its finish, how it's lit. Owners want the icons to belong to the
  character ("I like the style of the character").
- **The palette**: only the colours the owner picked, by hex. Three is enough: a dark, a light, one accent.
- **The type**: a rounded type wants rounded corners; a sharp serif can carry crisper shapes.
- **The grounds**: the icons must sit on the app's real background in light and dark.
- **What already works in the app**: the line-icon set stays for small UI, so the illustrated set has to live beside
  it.

## Showing them

1. Pick three test subjects that cover the range: one **state** (small, 24–28 pt), one **object** (36–48 pt), one
   **moment** (72 pt). For Comma: a draft state, a light bulb, a stamp.
2. Write one sample prompt per direction (the style paragraph plus a subject), and have Codex or ChatGPT make the
   three subjects in each.
3. Put all of them on one board page at their real sizes, light and dark, next to the line icons and the character.
   Never ask the owner to pick from a description.

## Four starting directions

Rewrite each in the brand's own words and colours. These are starting points, not a menu to copy.

**A · Flat graphic with subtle gradients.** Clean filled silhouettes, softly rounded corners, broad and very subtle
gradients, no strokes, front-facing with the slightest tilt, modest depth.
> Clean simple filled silhouettes, softly rounded corners, broad smooth very subtle gradients; <dark>, <light>,
> <accent>. Mostly front-facing, only the slightest natural tilt; modest depth. No strokes or outlines; interior lines
> are flat filled shapes. No grain, clay, plastic, inflated or puffy forms, bevels, gloss or highlights. Subject: a
> light bulb. One centred object, about 78% of a 1024 × 1024 transparent PNG, one very soft contact shadow.

**B · Duotone line-and-fill.** The line icons' stroke, grown up: the same stroke weight and corners, with one flat fill
in the accent. Closest to the existing UI; least risk next to small icons.
> A duotone icon: a <weight> rounded stroke in <dark>, one flat fill in <accent> behind part of the shape, no
> gradients, no shadow. Subject: a light bulb. Centred, about 70% of a 1024 × 1024 transparent PNG.

**C · Soft 3D game art.** Minimal stylised 3D, matte, lit from the top left, for apps with leagues, streaks and rewards.
> Minimal stylised 3D game art, matte, soft light from the top left, rounded but not inflated, <palette>, no gloss.
> Subject: a light bulb. One centred object, about 78% of a 1024 × 1024 transparent PNG, one very soft contact shadow.

**D · An open-licence set instead of generating.** For answer cards and emoji-like moments, a glossy 3D emoji set
under an open licence (Microsoft Fluent Emoji, MIT) can be enough, and costs nothing to make. Ship its licence.

## Recording the verdict

Keep a table in the brief or the app's taste notes, and never offer a rejected direction again:

| Direction | Verdict | Why (the owner's words) | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| A · flat graphic, subtle gradients | picked | "it looks absolutely bangers"; matches the character | <date> |
| Clay | rejected | the first clay batch was rejected outright | <date> |
| Puffy / inflated | rejected | went out with the clay batch: not the character's flat look | <date> |

The picked direction's sample prompt becomes the brief's style paragraph, word for word. The first icon the owner
approves in it becomes the second style reference.

## When the style is wrong for a size

If the picked style fails at a size (a stamp's detail lost at 28 pt), don't invent a second style. Simplify the
subject for that size, or keep that spot a line icon.
