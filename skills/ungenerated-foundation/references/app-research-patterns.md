# Patterns from top-grossing onboarding and paywall flows

Distilled notes, not screenshots. Each line is a mechanic worth borrowing; the look is always yours.

## Openings
- One hero object carries every beat; beats change around it (Brainrot, Opal, Headspace).
- The first screen names the outcome in a few words. No logo wall, no feature list.
- One hands-on moment early (drag, hold, tap the hero) makes it personal.
- 15–25 seconds before the first button; a tap moves to the next beat for people in a hurry.
- A before/after told visually: grey to colour, scattered to ordered, noisy to quiet.

## Questions
- One question per screen, a thin progress bar, the answer is the button (Brainrot).
- Vary the format: rows, tiles, a slider, a swipe deck, an app grid. Ten identical lists tire people.
- Questions earn the plan: each answer visibly shows up again later.
- No login before value. The account comes after the plan, before or after the paywall.

## Story
- A profile from the answers ("Your rhythm"), then the honest cost, then the good news.
- Numbers come from the person's own answers and are shown with their sum.
- Scale makes it land: a year, then ten years (Opal's years wheel, Brainrot's life in dots).
- The good news starts exactly where the bad news ended, in the same place.

## Plan and commitment
- The answers visibly become the plan (Brainrot's profile build).
- A small commitment before the price: hold to commit, sign, light something (Opal, Duolingo).

## Paywall
- One value screen right before the price, not three.
- Every price visible before any tap; the trial timeline honest: today, the reminder, the charge.
- Calm, bright, one colour field; no laurels, fake reviews or countdowns.

## Permissions and setup
- Explain in one line, then show the system sheet with a pointer to the button (Screen Time asks).
- Ask in context, never all at once at launch.
- Straight into doing after sign-up: the first action, not a tour of text (Duolingo's first lesson).
