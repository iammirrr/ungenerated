# What we learned, and why

The rules in this skill came from real rounds with one owner across several apps. Each lesson: the
rule, the reason (his words where he said it), and where it came from. Comma (a script-writing app),
Flamely (a focus tracker, phone and desktop), CutRoom (a video editor) and Pip are the apps. Their
exact looks are examples only; the rule is what transfers.

## The kit comes first

- **Make the kit before any screen: colours, type, buttons, press feel, grounds, motion.** "There
  should be a kit that is first being made that has button, animations, colours, everything." A friend
  who saw the board without a kit said the same thing first. (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Take the look from what the owner already has: their site, their brand, their desktop app.** Every
  invented palette was rejected; "look at my website, you will get a lot about me". A companion app
  traces every token to the product it accompanies. (CutRoom, Oct 2026; Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Copy the exact values of a look already picked; never approximate.** An invented peach gradient and
  plain dots replaced a chosen shade and pattern: "the colours are bad", "you didn't use the pattern
  that I chose". If a pick isn't recorded, say what you assumed. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Work the kit out back and forth, two or three options at a time, and lock each pick.** He chose by
  seeing, not by reading descriptions. (Comma, Flamely, Oct 2026)

## Colour, grounds and surfaces

- **Never the default AI kit: indigo or violet, the trendy serif-plus-grotesk pair, generic
  gradients, sparkles.** "AI slop colours"; "the default AI pairings are the giveaway". It became a
  lint rule. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Grounds are flat and calm by default.** "Why is the gradient changing?"; "I don't like
  this grid background"; "I do not like the smoke particles"; "I don't like the shines". He once asked
  for a soft gradient, then banned gradients; the later rule wins: flat, with one or two named
  exceptions (the primary button's gel, one hero sky). (Flamely, Comma, Oct 2026)
- **If a ground changes at all, it changes never within a flow.** A gradient that shifted between
  screens read as a cut. (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Far fewer shadows; none behind icons, logos or emoji.** "Overused shadows make things look less
  polished and less minimal"; "every icon has a shadow, which I do not like". Use hairlines and a soft
  inner glow instead. (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Premium is soft borderless surfaces on a warm or neutral ground.** No outlines, no hard gloss, no
  paper textures, tape or handwriting fonts: the paper-and-tape round was rejected: he wanted "an app feel".
  (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Prefer independent elements to boxed cards, and never double-outline.** "I do not like the boxed
  look ... I like independent look." (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Don't overuse any one colour.** "There is nothing wrong with brown, but AI uses brown excessively."
  Overuse is the tell, not the colour. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **One signature element, in one place.** A paper script sheet was loved; paper everywhere was hated.
  "If everything is unique, nothing in the app is." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **A slow-drifting pattern can carry identity, if AI tools don't default to it.** Swirling contour
  lines were his; dots and pluses are everyone's. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **The selected state never reuses a colour that already means something.** Green and yellow already
  meant two kinds of text, so a highlighter selection confused them. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Follow the phone's light or dark setting unless the owner sets a default.** Design both by hand.
  (Flamely, Oct–Nov 2026)

## Type

- **Pair one expressive headline face (with one accent word in italic) with a neutral sans.** "This
  font looks very aesthetically pleasing; it looks unique." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Plain, thick, readable type; no novelty display faces.** "I'm not a big fan of weird-looking fonts
  ... I like thick text." (CutRoom, Oct 2026)
- **Check custom fonts at the weights you use before showing anything.** Some rendered "wonky", and
  variable fonts defaulted to Light until the weight axis was set. (Flamely, Pip, Oct 2026)
- **No small text.** "No one is gonna read that, so you have to be very clear with the headings."
  (Flamely, Oct–Nov 2026)

## Shape

- **Corner radii stay consistent across nested shapes: inner = outer − padding.** "The rounded edges of
  the calendar doesn't look good at all." (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Radius is a pick, not a default.** One owner liked soft rounded cards in a phone app and wanted
  near-square corners in a pro desktop tool: "too much rounded radius". Ask. (Comma, CutRoom, Oct 2026)
- **Selected tiles never touch.** Keep a gap and draw the ring inside the tile, or picks merge into one
  blob. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Words never leak out of their shape.** A label crossing a line or pattern sits on a small pill.
  (Comma, Oct 2026)

## One job per screen

- **Each screen does one job and reads at a glance; cut everything else.** "The app is way too complex
  to even use. I made the app and I'm not able to understand it." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Compact by default.** Two related cards become two tabs of one card; secondary text collapses.
  "Compacting is the rule of the UX." He disliked expanding sections and preferred tabs. (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Far less text, more space; the chrome around the person's words is almost wordless.** "All of
  this is extremely text-heavy"; "remove little sub text please". (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **The first screens hold one idea each.** "There's like five logos in the onboarding." (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **One way to do each thing, one look per control.** Two add buttons were slop; one mic inside every
  text box, "so things are consistent". (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Use the platform's own navigation.** A text-only tab bar was "horrendous"; a custom bar of
  unlabelled glyphs was liked once, then reversed: "the previous navigation bar was pretty, glossy, how
  native apps do". Native wins. (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Don't dress the app up as something it isn't.** A weekday strip with no way to see other days was
  "a calendar app"; the job was a list of ideas. (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Home shows what to act on now,** not everything ever saved. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **An empty screen centres one useful action, never two.** "Anything that is empty, we have to give
  them action." (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Never type into a system alert inside a flow; type in place.** (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Ship the smallest version that does the core job.** "Decrease the bloat as much as possible."
  When a scope fails, cut back to what works. (CutRoom, Oct–Nov 2026)

## State and icons

- **A state is an icon, never a coloured row or a fraction.** "I'm not liking the blue and the weird
  orange stuff ... we should be representing that by icon"; "four of 15 beats? Why does it even show
  that?" (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Each kind of object gets one container style, used everywhere.** "We need to have the container
  for scripts, containers for films ... so I don't have to second-guess things." (Comma, Nov 2026)
- **Real apps get their real icon; things that aren't apps get an emoji or illustration, never a drawn
  tile.** "Google Docs is not an option here, it's not an icon." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **The whole identity mark is the hero, with nothing glowing behind it.** A bare flame was "just not
  our identity"; the full candle was. (Flamely, Oct 2026)

## Inputs

- **Where an input can be a toy, make it one:** a ruler under a needle, a bar chart you drag, a calendar
  that fills, a swipe deck. "I really love this age slider, wow." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Explain anything abstract with a tiny screen that plays it,** not an icon or a list. (Comma, Oct 2026)

## Words and numbers

- **Name every feature in words a newcomer understands.** "I don't know what draft leak is"; "Add to
  your notes. What do you mean by that?" (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Never label the person with a persona or category.** "Generalizing things that don't exist is what
  AI does." Talk about their time and their work. (Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Say the promise plainly; no corny lines.** "Stop posting into the void" was corny. (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Write like a person; if a line could be in any AI-made app, rewrite it.** (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Copy says something concrete; if the reader learns nothing, rewrite it.** "These scripts tell us
  nothing." (CutRoom, Nov 2026)
- **Frame the core action so nobody feels guilty using it.** "You are only borrowing their shape, not
  copying them." (Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Show only numbers the app can measure and that help a decision.** Exaggerate the scale, keep the
  maths honest; the person's own numbers beat template stats. (Flamely, Comma, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **No demo data. Run on a real day one and live content.** "The demo data is ruining the quality of
  everything." (Comma, Oct 2026)

## Research

- **Study the best apps in the category before designing a flow, and say that you did.** "Are you even
  looking at Brainrot? Are you using the Appllama MCP first?" (Flamely, Oct 2026)
- **Take intent and mechanics, never the look.** "Why are you becoming a copycat? Understand the
  intention." (Flamely, Oct 2026; Comma, Oct 2026)
- **Study references in depth and measure them.** Skimming a reference set the owner gave was called a
  failure; breaking reels down frame by frame gave hard numbers. (CutRoom, Oct–Nov 2026)
- **Put yours beside theirs.** The first redesign he liked came after laying his screen next to the
  category leader's. (CutRoom, Oct 2026)

## Naming

- **Pick a short one-word or character name, check the domain, and confirm before any rename.** He
  changed his mind on a rename within the hour. (Comma, Oct 2026)
