# Human copy

People read the heading, glance at one line, and tap. Write for that.

## Shape of a screen's words

- **Heading:** 2–6 words. One key word carries the accent: `Plan your *day.*`
- **One line under it**, 4–12 words, only if it adds something the heading cannot.
- **Button:** the action, 1–3 words. "Continue" until the last screen of a story; then the outcome
  ("Get started", "Make it my workday").
- **No paragraphs** on onboarding screens. If it needs a paragraph, it needs a picture.

## Rules

1. Say the specific thing. "5 hours a week" beats "more time". "Slack and email" beats "distractions".
2. The person's own numbers. Use their answers; show the sum so it can be checked.
3. Exaggerate the scale, never the facts: "2,600 hours over ten years" is the same maths as
   "260 a year", told bigger.
4. Second person, present tense, active voice.
5. One idea per screen.
6. Small print stays small and true ("An example day", "From your estimate").

## Never write

- "Unlock your potential", "supercharge", "seamless", "effortless", "elevate", "revolutionise",
  "game-changer", "journey", "empower", "in today's fast-paced world", "let's dive in".
- Questions as headings that the screen does not answer.
- Three adjectives in a row. Exclamation marks. Emoji in UI copy.
- "Simply", "just", "easily" before an instruction.
- Fake social proof ("Join 10,000+ happy users") unless it is true and measured.

## Errors and empty states

- Errors say what happened and what to do, in one line: "Give the project a name."
- Empty states say what will be here and the one action that fills it.

## Before you show

Read every screen's words aloud in order. If any line could appear in any other app unchanged,
rewrite it.
